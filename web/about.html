Outrage! Steal the Crown Jewels

2-6 thieves, 1 Tower of London, and 5 Crown Jewels that aren't just going to steal themselves now are they?

Setting the scene

To the unknowing onlooker, you're a simple tourist at the Tower of London, just one among a crowd of visitors. You have queued, you have paid, you have been handed a guidebook, and you are now shuffling along the wall walk in a slow line of visitors with cameras. The difference between you and the rest of these peasants?

You are going to steal the Crown Jewels.

A few weeks ago your family was invited to visit Prince Charles in Buckingham Palace, and as the youngest sibling of House Afeyan you were left to languish alone with the family nanny back in the Hills of Lexington. Never one to let a slight go unpunished, you have spent the last few weeks planning a heist that will make your family regret ever leaving you behind.

You arrive at the Tower today with your master plan in hand and a few tools in your pockets. It shouldn't be too difficult, all that's standing between you and the Crown Jewels is the paperwork, the Yeoman Warders, the ravens, the three towers they lock people in, and the Rack in the basement. Oh and did I forget to mention the other thieves who have decided today is the day they are going to steal the Crown Jewels too? I hope you remembered to bring your pistols. You will have to be quick, clever, and lucky if you want to get away with it.

The short version. Get signed in at Queen's House. Pick up a coin. Stroll into the White Tower, pocket a jewel, and walk out through the Cradle Tower. And make sure you do it before anybody else does.

Your tasks

1. Get accredited

You start outside on the wall walk, and while you are unaccredited you can only shuffle forwards along it with the rest of the crowd — no doubling back, no cutting inside. The wall walk dead-ends at Queen's House, where the clerks decide whether you are a person who belongs here. Pass, and the whole inner ward opens up: from then on you move freely in any direction.

2a. Get a coin

The Devereux Tower hands out coins, one per player. The coin is not treasure — it is the price of the exit. Without one, the door out of the Tower is just another square.

2b. Take a jewel

The jewels sit in a line inside the White Tower, which is one-way: you go in at one end and come out at the other, and nobody can shove you around while you are in there. Nobody can fight in there either. Standing on a jewel lets you try for it — a roll, made easier by whatever burglary tools you are carrying. Fail and you stay put and can try again next turn. There is no limit on attempts, only on time.

3. Get out — and come back

A jewel in your pocket is worth nothing. It can be taken off you in a fight, and it counts for nothing if the game ends while you are still carrying it. To make it count you have to walk out through the Cradle Tower, which costs you your coin and your entire hand of cards.

Leaving is not always the end of your game. You stash the jewels somewhere nothing can reach them, you are dealt a fresh hand, and you walk back in at the start square — still accredited, because the paperwork does not expire while you are out. Then you do it again.

Two ways to win

ModeHow it endsFeels like
Fast The first player to stash a single jewel wins immediately. A sprint. Half an hour, one good run, sudden death.
Slow Play continues until one player's stash is beyond catching, or until all five jewels have been stashed. Most jewels wins, with the grander jewels breaking ties. A campaign. Robbery, reprisal, and long memories.

What will go wrong

Almost everything, and mostly to somebody else, which is the point. Some of it you bring on yourself and some of it simply happens:

  • The ravens. Much of the inner ward is watched. Landing on a watched square turns over a raven card, face-down until you flip it, and it may send you anywhere from the gift shop to the Rack.
  • The Yeoman Warders. A manned post is a wall. You go the long way round, or you show a Disguise and walk straight past.
  • Other people. Land on an accredited rival outside the White Tower with a weapon in hand and you can start a fight. The winner takes their jewels, their coin, and a fresh handful of cards; the loser goes to hospital and loses a turn.
  • The cells. The Bloody Tower and the Bowyer Tower lock the door behind anyone who walks in. The Rack is worse: it takes your jewels and either your coin or your whole hand, and no amount of good rolling gets you off it early.
  • Three doubles. Roll three in a row and the guards take an interest. Bloody Tower.

Every one of these has a card that answers it, if you happen to be holding the right one at the right moment. Knowing which card is worth keeping and which is worth spending now is most of the skill.

More information

This is an online adaptation of the board game Outrage! Steal the Crown Jewels, built to be self-hosted: you run the server, you send your friends the link, and you all play in the browser. There are no accounts and no matchmaking — one server runs one game, and the game is saved as you go, so you can stop and pick it up later.

The rulebook has the detail, including every card in both decks. If you want to run a server or work on the code, the developer guide covers both.
